# Measure, Number, and Weight

*An essay presenting the vault's position as one continuous argument, structured like a dissertation in miniature: the assumptions, the state of research, what has been built, and the growth still to come. The short form of the position is [[What this project is]]; the to-do list is [[Future Directions]].*

**The thesis.** Mathematics should be rebuilt — not discarded — on three confessions: that its objects are real and created, that every claim in it needs an exhibited warrant, and that its scale is bounded by the creation it is part of. Each commitment costs something the modern discipline prefers not to pay. The claim of this essay is that what comes back is worth more.

## 1. The assumptions

**Divine revelation.** "Thou hast ordered all things in measure and number and weight" (Wisdom 11:20). The order mathematics studies is real because God made it; it is discovered, not invented; and it is *his* before it is ours. The vault states its premises rather than pretending to neutrality: the argument runs from text, to doctrine, to a philosophy of knowing, to mathematical commitment, with the strength of each layer stated openly ([[Theological Grounds]]). Mathematics is not religiously inert. The questions it answers worst — what a number is, what an equals sign means, how large the infinite looms — are the questions where theology was quietly doing the work all along.

**Human creatureliness.** God is infinite; we are not, and the difference is not a defect to be engineered away. We stand in the middle of the scale ([[Scale and Bands]]): unable to observe what takes centuries or a fraction of a millisecond, what is larger than our field of vision or smaller than a hair's width. Mathematics makes some of the invisible visible, but it is not a passport out of creaturehood. Our access to mathematical objects is real but partial: two fingers exhibit twoness completely; no one has ever exhibited $\pi$ — every presentation of it is an algorithm, a bound, a procedure ([[Creaturely Realism]]). Confessing this is epistemic humility, and the vault treats that humility as a duty, not a mood (Ps 131:1; [[Christians and the Reals]]).

**The historical lens.** Mathematics has a history that cannot be safely ignored. Kline's *Mathematics: The Loss of Certainty* is the vault's charter history: the nineteenth century did not merely add to mathematics, it changed what mathematics claimed to be, and the change was a loss registered as a gain. Read as postures rather than dates ([[Five Acts]]): receive, overreach, be exposed, deny, return chastened. The vault is an attempt at the fifth posture. History also supplies evidence at the level of practice: the Babylonians knew which divisions their system could perform exactly and which it could only approach, and said so; we have lost that honesty ([[Babylonian Mathematics]], [[Equal Signs]]).

## 2. The state of research

**Wildberger — the rational demonstration.** Norman Wildberger's rational trigonometry showed that a major field can be rebuilt exactly, with quadrance and spread in place of distance and angle, and no transcendental functions anywhere. His hyper-Catalan series solve general polynomial equations by an honest formal series; his finite-field Fourier theory recovers the classical circle integrals as the *same* rational numbers, with no continuum. The vault documents this work and checks it (it is tagged `third-party` throughout: [[Constructible Trigonometry]], [[Fundamental Theorem of Algebra]], [[Fourier Analysis]]). Its importance here is as proof of concept: exact reformulation is not austerity, it is fruitfulness.

**Poythress — the Christian ground.** Vern Poythress's *Redeeming Mathematics* argues that mathematical truth is grounded in the character of God, and his "equal ultimacy of the one and the many" already does work in this vault ([[Theology of Number]]). The engagement is begun, not finished; a fuller literature check is open work ([[Future Directions]] item 10).

**The ultrafinitists and constructivists.** Esenin-Volpin asked where the numbers run out; Nelson questioned whether arithmetic itself is consistent and built probability on finite, radically elementary grounds; Parikh and Sazonov formalized *feasibility* — the difference between a finite procedure and an executable one. Bishop's constructive analysis showed that the working theorems survive when every existence claim must produce a witness. And physics itself puts a ceiling on computation in this universe (the Bekenstein and Margolus–Levitin bounds; the figures are order-of-magnitude and flagged for verification on [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]). The vault's difference from these neighbors is its ground: it constructs not because intuition demands it but because creatures before an infinite God may not claim more than they can exhibit — and it names the cost of every claim, explicitly, as a certificate.

## 3. The directions — what has been built

**The word "number," split.** The word *number* today is like the word *star* two thousand years ago: planets, stars and galaxies under one name. The vault sorts them into a fourteen-level hierarchy with four kinds: **Numbers** (exhibited, decidable by a finite check), **Lengths** (constructed: compass, straightedge, the fold), **Pixels** (computed to a stated resolution, equality meaning agreement at that resolution), and **Posits** (asserted by convention, not exhibited — recorded on the ladder not because they are warranted but so that the believer in the completed reals must face what his acceptance asserts: uncomputable numbers without any exhibition). Trust is a gradient across the levels, and the levels exist because the gradient is real ([[Number Hierarchy]], [[Numbers, Lengths and Pixels]]).

**Certificates.** A certificate is a procedure, and each kind of "=" is the procedure its level affords ([[Certificates]]). Before any equals sign, the question is: *what is your manner of proof?* Exhibit, construct, converge with a stated error, or name a convention — four different verbs that classical mathematics bills under one symbol.

**Constructible trigonometry.** Fifteen triangle cases solved exactly, plus area, inradius and circumradius — with slopes instead of angle measure and no transcendental functions ([[Constructible Trigonometry]]); and where sine and cosine would go, a discrete rational orbit that stays exactly on the circle forever ([[Rational Rotation]]).

**Constructible statistics.** Statistics rebuilt on a constitution: every statistical object finitely specified, every numerical result constructible by an explicit procedure, every claim supported by a finite certificate. Regression is the pillar (the Pythagorean decomposition of total quadrance into explained and residual, in exact fractions); the central shape is the family $\binom nk/2^n$ — the Pascal distributions — to the exclusion of the Gaussian; design-based probability is the foundation, because its probabilities are grounded in an actual finite randomization that can be enumerated and counted; and Bayesian inference is the primary inferential framework in a specifically finite form, where every prior must carry a justification ([[Constructible Statistics]]).

**The change ladder.** Calculus done honestly: discrete calculus first (exact, finite, algebraic), then a first-order calculus with $\varepsilon^2=0$, and the five great theorems each given a budget form — the intermediate value theorem by bisection with a modulus, the extreme value theorem on a grid, the mean value theorem as an inequality, Bolzano–Weierstrass as pigeonhole and metastability, the fundamental theorem with its finite form as the proved theorem. The classical theorems are not denied; their price is itemized ([[Discrete Calculus]], [[Calculus]], [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]]).

**Pedagogy.** A teaching framework for all of it: the Quadriga — question, sight, practice, demonstration ([[Quadriga for Mathematics]]) — and a curriculum in historical order ([[Historical Order]]), in which arithmetic is a skill that can be drilled and timed, while mathematics proper is an art and a science that cannot. For the majority of students, whose last mathematics is statistics, the end track is the constructible statistics above.

## 4. Areas for future growth

The honest list is the Agenda itself ([[Future Directions]]), kept current as the vault changes. The core open problems, as of this writing:

- **The threshold as a function.** Creational ultrafinitism's bound should be a resource-relative, provable notion of feasibility — the vault's hardest and most central problem.
- **Derivatives with certificates.** The derivatives of $\exp$, $\log$, sine and cosine, taken from their discrete ancestors with explicit error — the largest unlock in the calculus strand.
- **The bridge theorem.** A general bound licensing the move from the discrete to the infinitesimal calculus.
- **The logic and the induction.** What proof is in the vault's three-valued logic, and what induction certifies when each application of the step has a cost.
- **The Reals.** A page defining the classical target precisely, so the thesis has something exact to aim at.
- **The literature check.** Whether the position's combination — theologically grounded, certificate-explicit, ultrafinite — is in fact new, must be established by search, not asserted.

Which claims are settled and which are open is not a matter of tone: the Settled table in the Agenda is the whole list of what is settled, and everything else is open work, flagged rather than smoothed over.

## The invitation

The vault is public. Its pages hold only the working mathematics; its history lives in its log; its decisions and their authority are marked. The wager of the project is that mathematics loses nothing it needs by confessing its measure — and gains its warrant back.
