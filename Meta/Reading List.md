# Reading List

Books to have read before publishing this program. **Every title, publisher and year below is from memory: verify the edition before citing.** Entries are marked:

- **★** read before publishing (the claim it bears on will be attacked or compared against it);
- **◆** should read;
- **○** reference or source to consult;
- **[2020]** already in Murphy's 2020 bibliography (his c2020 Christian Mathematics essay);
- the vault page(s) the book bears on.

The purposes are three: **know the prior art** (the novelty claim of [[Future Directions]] item 10 cannot be made without it), **know the opposition** in its own words, and **have the primary sources** for the historical claims. Selection beyond Murphy's named authors is **[AI]**.

## 1. Morris Kline (nearly everything)

- ★ *Mathematics: The Loss of Certainty* (Oxford, 1980) [2020]. The spine of the history ([[Five Acts]], [[Corrective Narrative]]).
- ★ *Mathematical Thought from Ancient to Modern Times* (Oxford, 1972; three volumes) [2020]. The reference for the history table in [[Historical Order]].
- ◆ *Mathematics in Western Culture* (Oxford, 1953). Your 2020 bibliography lists a title *Mathematics in Western Thought* (Oxford, 1966); I recall the title as above, so check which is meant.
- ◆ *Mathematics: A Cultural Approach* (Addison-Wesley, 1962) [2020].
- ◆ *Mathematics and the Physical World* (1959); ◆ *Mathematics and the Search for Knowledge* (Oxford, 1985): the physical grounding of number, bearing on [[Number in Natural Philosophy]] and [[Scale and Bands]].
- ★ *Why Johnny Can't Add: The Failure of the New Math* (1973; reprinted by Vintage) [2020] and ★ *Why the Professor Can't Teach: Mathematics and the Dilemma of University Education* (St. Martin's, 1977) [2020]: the pedagogy ([[Curriculum Plan]], [[Corrective Narrative]]).
- ◆ *Calculus: An Intuitive and Physical Approach* (Wiley, 1967; Dover) and ○ *Mathematics for the Nonmathematician* (1967): Kline's own teaching of the calculus and of the general reader's mathematics; compare [[Calculus]].

## 2. John L. Bell and Paul Lockhart

**Bell** ([[Infinitesimals]], [[Calculus]], [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]])

- ★ *A Primer of Infinitesimal Analysis* (Cambridge, 1998; 2nd ed. 2008): smooth infinitesimal analysis, the arithmetic of $\varepsilon^2=0$.
- ★ *The Continuous and the Infinitesimal in Mathematics and Philosophy* (Polimetrica, 2005): the history of the continuum and the case for infinitesimals.
- ◆ *The Art of the Intelligible* (Kluwer, 1999): an elementary survey of mathematics in its conceptual development.
- ○ "Continuity and Infinitesimals", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (online).

**Lockhart** ([[Quadriga for Mathematics]], [[Historical Order]], [[Curriculum Plan]])

- ★ *A Mathematician's Lament* (Bellevue Literary Press, 2009).
- ◆ *Measurement* (Harvard, 2012) and ◆ *Arithmetic* (Harvard, 2017): his teaching of geometry-as-measurement and of arithmetic, directly comparable to the vault's sequence.

- ★ The physical bounds: J. D. Bekenstein, "Universal upper bound on the entropy-to-energy ratio for bounded systems" (*Physical Review D* 23, 1981); S. Lloyd, "Computational capacity of the universe" (*Physical Review Letters* 88, 2002); N. Margolus and L. Levitin, "The maximum speed of dynamical evolution" (*Physica D* 120, 1998). ## Search questions for the literature check ([[Future Directions]] item 10)

These are searches, not books. Each should either find prior art, which must then be cited, or come back empty, which may then be said with the search recorded.

1. A Christian philosophy of mathematics that **rejects or limits actual infinity** on theological grounds, beyond Craig, Aquinas, Bonaventure, Poythress and Nickel.
2. **Constructible trigonometry** (slope as primitive, closed under square roots, no angle measure), set against Wildberger's rational trigonometry.
3. **Statistics taught in quadrance and exact counts** from the start.
4. **Discrete calculus taught before limits** at the secondary level, beyond Zeilberger and Concrete Mathematics.
5. Treatments of **exact symbolic output with a quarantined transcendental** as a principle of computation, beyond computer-algebra practice.
6. **Ultrafinitism with a physical bound**, beyond Parikh, Nelson, Sazonov.
7. **Ethical and theological arguments for epistemic humility about mathematical infinity.**
