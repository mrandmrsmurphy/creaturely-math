# Counting and Bases (the bottom of the hierarchy, taught)

The Confession is the most complete statement of *how the number types arise from need*, in historical order, with each extension tied to a real-world analogue — and with creaturely honesty at each step.

## Matching → counting → bases

The tally stage in detail: [[Tally Marks]].

1. **Matching** (bijection between collections) is "more fundamental than numbering." Then matching to body parts (fingers), then to tally marks.
2. **Bundling** tally marks into bundles of bundles = a **number base**. Base ten is common; "two, eight, sixteen, and sixty should also be learned." (Two, eight, sixteen: [[Binary]]; sixty: [[Sexagesimal]]; and with negative digits, [[Balanced Ternary]]. Remainders after bundling: [[Modular Arithmetic]].)
3. **Operations**: *concatenation* first (young students saying $2+3=23$ "are not wrong with respect to tally marks!"). Commutativity, associativity.
4. **Counting numbers are ordered; each has a successor — "taken on faith, which cannot be proven."** "As finite creatures, we cannot count beyond some (very large) number." → [[Creational Ultrafinitism]].
5. **Duality**: area = repeated addition = multiplication — "equivalence between a concept from one area… and another."
6. **Inverses → new numbers**: subtraction fails in ℕ → zero ("distinguished from 'impossible'") → negatives (debt, reverse direction); division fails → ℚ.
7. Multiplicative inverses ⇒ organise ℚ around 1 not 0 ⇒ **exponentiation** ("family of fours": 1, 4, 16, 64…), then rational exponents ("sometimes works: $4^{1/2}=2$; much more often… impossible to complete fully") ⇒ square roots (duality with squares) ⇒ **logarithms**: $2^3=8$, $\log_28=3$, $\sqrt[3]8=2$ are "three ways to write every sentence involving exponents."
8. 16th c.: $\sqrt{-1}$ → imaginary/"lateral" numbers → ℂ. 17th c.: Newton/Leibniz need "a successor to zero": **ε with ε² = 0** — infinitesimals; "we are incapable of distinguishing among infinitesimals, except to say one is zero and that there are others." → [[Infinitesimals]].

**π and exhaustion:** "Human beings are incapable of exhaustively exhibiting the value of π… no real-world application requires exhaustive knowledge." Conics and elliptic curves: "a more convoluted topic than human beings are capable of exhausting… unending processes which get better and better."

## The Babylonian honesty about ⅐

60 = 2²·3·5, so $\frac12,\frac13,\frac14,\frac15,\frac16$ "exist" exactly in base 60, but $\frac17$ does not. In sexagesimal $\frac17=0;\overline{8,34,17}$ ([[Sexagesimal]]) — the Babylonians wrote $\frac{8}{60}+\frac{34}{60^2}+\frac{17}{60^3}$ and **stopped**, even though they could see it repeat; "who is even chopping stuff up into 216,000 pieces?" and left $\frac17$ out of the reciprocal tables. "To them, math was about what you could actually do." "When is it appropriate or possible to divide by seven?" — a question "never had in math classes… too theological and philosophical."

**Contrast:** our $\overline{142857}$ bar "means *repeat forever*" — "we imagine we can master a seventh."

Fraction termination rule: $\frac1n$ terminates in base $b$ iff every prime factor of $n$ divides $b$. (Base 10: only 2 and 5.) The cake can be cut into eleven *approximately* equal pieces; a dollar cannot be shared equally among three friends.
