# Constructible Numbers 𝕂 (levels 5–6 of the [[Number Hierarchy]])

> "We begin where every honest inquiry must begin: with what we can actually do. Not what we can imagine doing, not what we can describe in symbols, but what we can perform with finite steps and exhibit to another finite mind. This is the constructible field 𝕂, and it is smaller than the modern mathematician pretends. But it is exact." — Constructible Trigonometry, Introduction

## Definition

𝕂 = all numbers obtainable from 0 and 1 by finitely many applications of $+,-,\times,\div,\sqrt{\ }$. Every rational is in 𝕂; $\sqrt5$, $\frac{1+\sqrt5}2$, $\sqrt{2+\sqrt3}$ are; $\sqrt[3]2$ is not.

**Why $\sqrt[3]2\notin\mathbb K$:** any element of 𝕂 satisfies a polynomial whose degree is a power of 2 (tower of quadratic extensions; Galois group a 2-group). $\sqrt[3]2$ has degree 3. "This is not a limitation to mourn. It is a boundary to respect." "A theorem of field theory, not merely an observation."

- **Trisecting the angle / doubling the cube** — the cubic wall.
- **π ∉ 𝕂**, and not even algebraic: "maximally distant from exact exhibition." A 2024 image: $\sqrt[3]2$ is "like a person with a reach of 7′ trying to touch a 7′6″ ceiling," but π "is not even in the room."
- **Artin's example:** $x^5-x-1=0$ has no solution in radicals — "grant the infinity of all numbers written with arithmetic and any exponents, you still cannot describe its roots."
- **Origami** (Alperin): folding allows trisection and cube roots; "history could've gone differently" — ℂ was originally just a way to extend 𝕂 to solve cubics. Level 6.
- **Teaching chain** (article): $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Q\to\mathbb K\to\mathbb A\to\mathbb R$ (Precioso 2011, p. 172) — Murphy's argument is that 𝕂 and 𝔸 should be *inserted* in the school sequence before ℝ.
- **Square-with-side-1:** the diagonal is exhibited and $\in\mathbb K$ — [[Equal Signs]] discusses how that squares with "equality is asymptotic" at level 5.

## Constructible versus operational vs algorithmic

ℚ and 𝕂 are *operational*: each number is produced by finite steps from 0 and 1. ℝ is *algorithmic*: its numbers are produced by infinite processes. "ℝ is not a field in the same sense; it is a *completion*, a closing of the brackets on infinite processes. To treat ℝ as operational is to treat a **promise as a possession**."
