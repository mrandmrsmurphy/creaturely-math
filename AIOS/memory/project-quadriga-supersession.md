Murphy substantially disagrees with his friend Phillip Johnson, who advocates for the Quadrivium. Murphy holds that it has been superseded. His Quadriga directly challenges the Platonic ideal of mathematical contemplation as an ascent toward the divine or the forms and a detachment from material life. He affirms good contemplation.

Murphy reads the Scientific Revolution as evidence for this thesis and as kin to the Protestant Reformation and good humanism. He characterizes its challenge in his own words: “Scholastic contemplation without measurement is sub-human.” Language is prior, better, and first. The Trivium precedes quadrivial knowledge. Number is God's primary means of communicating about the material universe and many ideas and is central to our understanding of them.

The decisive omission in the inherited Quadrivium is praxis, the skilled doing essential to problem solving and symbolic thought. Murphy sets Plato’s disparagement of practical mathematics as shopkeepers’ work beside Jesus as a *tekton*. He wants to advocate the Quadriga throughout classical education, Christian and secular.

**How to apply:** Treat supersession as a major thesis of Murphy’s. Preserve the place of good contemplation and the priority of language. Treat the formulation about scholastic contemplation as Murphy’s characterization and check the historical details before publication.
