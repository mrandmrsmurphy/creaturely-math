"A Catechism of Mathematics

What is multiplication? On the one hand, it is repeated addition.  At the same time, it is area.
And repeated multiplication? This is – at the same time – exponentiation, and adding a dimension.  For example, length times width is area, and area times height is volume.
What is the area of a triangle? By visual inspection, a right triangle is half its rectangle, which was base times height.
Are all triangles right? No, they might also be acute – with angles smaller than right, or obtuse – with one angle greater than right.
What is the area of an acute triangle? By dropping an altitude, we see that it is the sum of two right triangles.  Therefore, it’s area is also one-half base times height.
And the area of an obtuse triangle? Bizarre as it might seem, if we extend the original rectangle, we see the same formula holds.
What can we generalize from this experiment? The area of slanted object is the same as its unslanted original.  
Give me another example. The area of a parallelogram is the same as that of it’s corresponding rectangle.
Does this continue into other dimensions? Yes.  A shape with a consistent cross-section has a volume equal to the area times the non-slant height.
Can we always find the slant-height of a given triangle? Yes and no.  There is a formula, but it is for the square of the slant-height.
Give the slant-height formula for a right triangle. The sum of the squares of the legs of a right triangle is equal to the square of the hypotenuse.
Can we always square-root a given number? The square root of a given number can be approximated to within a specified accuracy, but it cannot always be exhibited.
So, not all numbers belong to the same genus? Correct.  Some numbers correspond to whole quantities, some to ratios or fractions, and some require infinite processes.
Does these species of quantities exist among lengthes and angles? No they do not.  Any angle can be bisected and any length can be used as a square-area.
Can a given angle be trisected? No, an arbitrary angle cannot be trisected without access to a higher dimension.
What is a circle? A circle is the set of all points a given distance – called the radius – from its center.
What kind of numbers are neded to describe the circuference of a circle? The circumference of a circle is incommensurate with its radius.  It is not a constructable number.
What is the method for calculating the circumference of a circle? The circumference is equal to the diameter – twice the radius – times the unlistable number pi.
What is the beginning of pi? Pi begins as 3.14159…  It can be reasonably approximated as 355 over 113.
Can we find the area of a circle? If we cut a circle into smaller and smaller wedges, we could lay it out as a better and better rectangle.  The length would be half the circumference and the height a radius.  Therefore, the area of a circle is pi times the radius squared.
How are right-triangles and circles related? A circle can be thought of as all the possible triangles with one non-right angle at the center, the hypotenuse fixes, and the legs allowed to vary freely.
What does modern mathematics declare a line to be? A line is said to be all the points in one dimension.  It is believed to include all possible distances, whether whole, rational, or irrational amount away from the center, either to the left or to the right.
What is the center of such a number line? We typically count off places on a number line by adding one to step rightward.  In this case, the center is at zero.
Is zero a number? We distinguish between one less than one – which we call zero, and non-number answers – which we call ‘undefined’.
If we can go left of 1 on the number line, can we go left of zero? Yes.  We name the number one unit left of zero as negative one.  The left half of the number line is the negative reflection of the right half.
On the number line, what are addition and subtraction? On the number line, addition is stepping rightward, while subtraction is stepping leftward.
So, what are negative numbers? Negative numbers can represent loss or deficit, but they are also additive inverses of their positive counterparts.  For example, subtracting 3 is the same as adding negative 3.
Does every number have an additive inverse? Yes, every number has a unique additive inverse, but zero is it’s own additive inverse.
