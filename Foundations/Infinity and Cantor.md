# Infinity and Cantor

Companion to [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]. **Cantor is not rejected; he is re-read.**

## What is accepted

- **Bijection** ($n\mapsto 2n$ pairs ℕ with the evens) — illuminating *as a structural observation*: *if* both could be formed as completed sets they would be "the same size."
- **The diagonal argument** — no list of the reals exists. Accepted as a **negative result**. It makes some kind of sense when reasoning about what God could conceive; as a claim about numbers in our human sense it is incoherent ([[Creaturely Realism]]). "A flaming sword east of that territory": receive the diagonal as a *limit-marker*, not an invitation to collect what lies beyond it.

## What is refused

The **ontological conclusion**: that ℕ and ℝ exist as *completed objects*. "Closing the bracket after the dots" claims possession of an actually infinite totality. "The Axiom of Infinity is a theological claim disguised as a mathematical axiom." (ZFC and large cardinals are the top two rungs of the logic ladder — see [[Structure Hierarchies]].)

## What the diagonal says in the vault

The diagonal is a **procedure** and its conclusion is a **negative about lists**: no listing procedure exhausts the sequences (worked out on [[Diagonal Argument]]). The whole numbers can be generated in order; the sequences cannot be generated in any order. That is the difference the vault keeps, with no completed set and no cardinal number.

## Even if you accept the reals: ℵ₀ versus 𝔠, read through certificates

*This section grants the standard picture, in which the reals are a completed totality, in order to show that the position survives even there.* The vault itself does not hold that picture ([[Number Hierarchy]]).

| Collection | Size | Certificate per member |
|---|---|---|
| ℕ, ℤ, ℚ | ℵ₀ (listable) | **Finite certificate for every member**: the count, the integer, the reduced pair. Each is creaturely-accessible one by one. |
| ℝ (non-terminating decimals) | 𝔠 (not listable) | **Most members have none.** No listing procedure of finitely describable sequences is complete ([[Diagonal Argument]]) — a statement about procedures, not a count of a completed totality. ("Definable" is not itself definable without paradox: the Berry/Richard worry.) |

Granting the reals, the diagonal argument **confirms** the position: you cannot even certify the reals *sequentially*, let alone completely. This is the "Babel shape": reaching for a totality no creature can hold. See [[Number Hierarchy]], levels 7–12, and [[Equal Signs]].

*Without* the grant, the second row's reading "ℝ is not countable, so most reals are undefinable" would presuppose a completed ℝ larger than the definable numbers. The procedural statement in the table needs no such totality.

## The Continuum Hypothesis: independent of the standard axioms

Cantor's Continuum Hypothesis (CH) cannot be settled by the standard axioms of set theory (ZFC, if ZFC is consistent). Gödel (1940) showed that ZFC cannot **disprove** it. Paul Cohen (1963) showed that ZFC cannot **prove** it. So CH is **independent** of ZFC, and the same pair of results holds for the Axiom of Choice relative to ZF. Independence is a different result from Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931): CH is an example of the kind of statement those theorems say must exist, but it was settled by different methods.

The historical setting (Cantor, Dedekind, Kronecker and the foundations debates) is on [[Mathematics 1730 to 1900]].

## Earlier statement (2020)

The Christian Mathematics essay told the story more dramatically: Cantor was repeatedly institutionalized; Hilbert: "we do not wish to be expelled from the paradise of Cantor's theory of sets"; Kline's *Loss of Certainty* for the "disastrous aftermath." The *argument* survives into the 2026 position.
