# Four Kinds of Truth

> Murphy's list, from the *Hamartological* mark of the [[Quadriga for Mathematics]]: "Science can produce truth, but it is the most provisional, least reliable kind of truth. There are four."

1. **Revelation**: God's self-disclosure.
2. **Properly basic**: truths that need no outside justification, including the *sensus divinitatis*.
3. **Logical**: "All bachelors are unmarried men."
4. **Verisimilitudes**: true as far as we know, subject to later revision.

**Natural observations are data for verisimilitudes** (Murphy). So observation is not a fifth kind of truth; it is the evidence from which the fourth kind is built.

## Why the fourth kind is the least reliable, and why that is an honour

The wiki that reports the source reads the list as running from the most to the least certain and revisable, and says the placing of empirical science in the fourth category "is not a demotion but a description": scientific truth is real but provisional, the most self-correcting, which is precisely why it works. The theological reason is the **Hamartological** one: total depravity means no mind or generation can overcome the noetic effects of sin, so inquiry must be multi-generational and checkable by others ("I am not trustworthy alone"). Provisionality is the institutional acknowledgment of that limit, not a defect.

## What this does for mathematics

The source applies the four kinds to science. The vault's mathematics uses the same list, and doing so sorts several things that would otherwise be loose.

| Kind | In the vault | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Revelation** | The ground of the theological premises of the position | God alone is infinite; creatures are finite and ordered; "measure and number and weight" ([[Theological Grounds]], [[Theology of Number]]) |
| **Properly basic** | The starting point of realism and of the arithmetic one cannot argue anyone into | That there are numbers to discover; that each number has a successor *as a process we can carry on* ([[Creaturely Realism]], [[First Principles of Thought]]). *Which* of the vault's first principles are properly basic, and which are only convenient, is Murphy's call. |
| **Logical** | A theorem, relative to a stated system | $6!=720\equiv-1\pmod7$; Wilson, Fermat, Euler; "no ratio squares to $2$" ([[Wilson's Theorem]], [[Diagonal Argument]]); Success = *correctness* in the sense of [[Three Tiers]] |
| **Verisimilitude** | Every claim that a mathematical system *describes* something measured; and every claim about **where the line falls** | A length is a number with a tolerance ([[Number in Natural Philosophy]]); the $\approx10^{200}$ threshold of [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] is derived from physical estimates (the Bekenstein bound with rough inputs), so *the number is a verisimilitude, while the principle it illustrates, that creatures are finite before an infinite God, is not*. |

Consequences worth stating:

- **A certificate is the machinery that moves a claim from "asserted" to "logical" within a system** ([[Certificates]]). What a certificate cannot do is promote a verisimilitude: an exact rational for a measured length is still a model of a measurement.
- **The three-part position carries three different strengths.** Creaturely realism leans on the properly basic; certificate-explicit constructivism is a discipline for the logical kind; creational ultrafinitism has a principle (revelation, with philosophy) and a figure (verisimilitude). Confusing the strengths is what lets an opponent say "you cannot be certain of $10^{200}$": true of the figure, not of the principle.
- **Applied mathematics** sits mostly in the fourth kind. A well-posed *Quaestio* includes saying which kind of truth an answer will deliver ([[Quaestio]]).

## Hard problems

1. **Where do mathematical theorems sit?** The source's own example of the logical is a definitional truth ("bachelors are unmarried men"), which is true by the structure of the concepts. Whether a deep theorem is "logical" in that sense, or something to be put with the properly basic or with a logical truth *relative to axioms chosen*, is not settled.
2. **Where does the position itself sit?** The vault's central thesis is a mixture of the kinds. A reader should be able to see which sentences are which; [[Christians and the Reals]] does this by claim A and claim B and could be extended to name the kind of each premise.
