# Christians and the Reals

The thesis Murphy wants to push: **Christians should not accept the Reals.** This page states what that can mean, the argument, how strong each step is, the evidence gathered in the vault, the objections, and what is still missing.

## The ethical form of the thesis (Murphy)

The "should" is **ethical**: Christians *ought* not to claim to be able to carry out infinite processes, or to know what "happens at infinity." God enjoins greater epistemic humility on creatures. Secular mathematicians say they can conceive of $10^{200}$; in fact they imagine that they can imagine it. The leap from *imagining it* to *imagining that I can imagine it* is the pride Scripture forbids.

**Texts for a duty of epistemic humility** (references to check): Ps 131:1 (not occupying myself with things too great for me), Job 38:2 and 42:3 (words without knowledge; things too wonderful), Rom 12:3 (think with sober judgment), 1 Tim 1:7 (confident assertions about what one does not understand), 1 Tim 6:20 (what is falsely called knowledge), 1 Cor 8:2, Prov 26:12, Jas 4:16, and Gen 3:5 (the first temptation was epistemic: to know as God knows).

**What makes this the strongest form**: it is a claim about the **speech act**, not about metaphysics. It is claim **A** of [[Theological Grounds]] (no warrant to assert) read as a *virtue*. It does not need claim B and it does not depend on the exact threshold: "we can debate the biggest number" is compatible with the duty.

**Where it must be aimed carefully**:

1. **Reasoning about a large number is not the vice.** One can reason about $10^{200}$ by its description ($664$ bits; arithmetic by rule), as one reasons about a trillion without picturing it. The vault grants this in the meta-level/object-level distinction. The pride lies in the **object-level claim**: that one *holds* the items (a list of that many things), or *knows what happens* at the end of a process that has no end. A fair statement: the vice is claiming possession or survey, not using a notation.
2. **The Bekenstein bound supports the object-level point, and only that.** It bounds holding $N$ separate items: about $10^{123}$–$10^{124}$ bits for the observable universe, so a list of $10^{200}$ is out of reach in principle ([[Creational Ultrafinitism]]). It does not forbid the 664-bit description. State it as a bound on *enumeration*.
3. **Aim at the claim, not the person.** Many classical mathematicians mean a structural or formal statement, not a mental picture, by "there are $10^{200}$ of them" or "the set of reals." The charge of pride is just only where "exists" is read as **possession**; elsewhere it misdescribes them, and a Christian reader will notice (cf. Mt 7:1–5 on judging motives). Offer a test instead: *What would count as a warrant for what you are claiming?*
4. **The apophatic parallel.** Christians themselves say "God is infinite" while confessing that he is incomprehensible: infinity is said as a **negation** (not bounded), not as a survey. The vault's *potential* infinity is the same move for numbers: "there is no last one," not "here is the whole." That shows the duty is not hostile to the word *infinite*, and it may be the most persuasive way to put it.

## First: what is "accepting the Reals"?

"The Reals" is four different things, and the verdict differs for each:

| What "the Reals" means | Verdict in the vault |
|---|---|
| **(a) A notation / idealization**: use $\pi$, $\sqrt2$, $e$, decimal expansions, limits, with known error | **Allowed**, when the certificate is named (levels 5–11 of [[Number Hierarchy]]) |
| **(b) Constructive reals**: rational Cauchy sequences with a modulus; computable reals | **Allowed** as certificates of numbers at levels 7–11 (the Bishop-style neighbour in [[Schools Compared]]) |
| **(c) The classical completed ℝ**: a complete ordered field, every bounded set has a least upper bound, uncountably many members | **Not accepted as a collection of numbers**: it is a completed infinite totality, most of whose members have no certificate ([[Infinity and Cantor]]) |
| **(d) Reals as divine ideas**: God knows every digit of $\pi$ and every real | **Accepted as the vault's realism allows**: real, not a *number* in our sense ([[Creaturely Realism]]) |

So the thesis is not "no π" and not "no real analysis". The sharp claim is about **(c)**, and about equating (c) with (a)–(b): the equivocation the vault calls **bamboozlement** ([[Certificates]]). A fair statement of the thesis: **a Christian should not assert the classical completed ℝ as a system of numbers, and should not use one "=" across levels without naming the certificate.**

### The internal argument (steps 3 and 4)

This is the strongest step and it belongs to Murphy's own position, which a Platonist cannot use. A Platonist can place ℝ in a separate realm or in God's mind and leave creation untouched. Creaturely realism says mathematical objects are *creational*. If the creation contains no actualized infinity (2), then the completed ℝ is not among the created mathematical objects. What is left for it to be is either a **divine idea** (fine, but then not a *number* in our sense, which is already the vault's position) or a **human fiction** (which Murphy rejects for number). So the conclusion lands exactly where the vault already is.

### The three places a number can live

[[Creaturely Realism]] says mathematical objects are creational *and* that God could conceive of a Real. Are numbers (i) **divine ideas** (God's archetypal knowledge, unbounded), (ii) **created quantities** (the number of stars, a length, a ratio in a creature), or (iii) **our constructions** (certificates)? The argument needs the answer, because infinity is acceptable in (i) and, by premise 2, not in (ii). The answer: all three exist; "number" in the human sense is (ii) and (iii); the Reals live in (i).

## Evidence file: where the classical ℝ is used, and what survives

Each theorem that leans on completeness has been worked through. **The pattern is uniform**: where the exact statement needs a completed limit or supremum, an *approximate form with a stated modulus and cost* remains; where the theorem is finite and algebraic, it stays whole.

| Theorem | Needs completeness? | Result |
|---|---|---|
| [[Fundamental Theorem of Calculus]] | no (finite form is telescoping) | kept; infinitesimal global half flagged |
| [[Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic]], [[Chinese Remainder Theorem]], [[Fermat's Little Theorem]], [[Euler's Theorem]], [[Wilson's Theorem]] | no | adopted whole |
| [[Fundamental Theorem of Algebra]] | yes (roots in $\mathbb C$) | exact rejected; budget form with isolating-interval certificate |
| [[Intermediate Value Theorem]] | yes | exact rejected (constructed failure); approximate form, **linear** in digits |
| [[Extreme Value Theorem]] | yes | value findable, **location not**; cost $\propto1/\varepsilon$ |
| [[Mean Value Theorem]] | via EVT | inequality form proved without EVT; point form approximate |
| [[Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem]] | yes | exact rejected; **fails computably** (Specker); pigeonhole and metastable forms |
| Cantor's diagonal ([[Diagonal Argument]]) | no | accepted as a procedural negative |

The technical heart is the **Specker sequence**: a computable, bounded, monotone sequence of exactly known rationals whose limit is not computable. So the completeness axiom of the classical ℝ asserts the existence of limits for which no certificate can exist even from perfect rational data (derivation checked on [[Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem]]; the attribution to Specker is a recollection). That is a mathematical fact about what ℝ *adds*, and it is exactly what a creature cannot hold.

## Objections to meet

1. **Cantor was a Christian and defended the transfinite as in the mind of God** (his "Absolute Infinite"); Augustine (*City of God* XII.19, to check) says the series of numbers is comprehended by God. *Reply:* granted, and consistent with (d): God's knowledge of the infinite. The thesis is about what **creatures possess**, and about the word *number*. The vault's place for the infinite in God's mind is explicit ([[Creaturely Realism]]).
2. **The Reals work: physics and engineering.** *Reply:* every applied result uses finite precision. The evidence file shows exact classical theorems recovered as ε-forms; the cost is that some (the EVT) are exponentially dearer in digits. What is not yet shown: that *all* applied analysis goes through. Not claimed. Fourier is begun: [[Fourier Analysis]] — integration over the circle has a finite, rational avatar (Limanta–Wildberger), and the FFT was always a finite computation. Statistics is begun too: [[Constructible Statistics]] does the working core — finite datasets, exact rational and constructible certificates, explicit finite bounds. And differential equations in practice are **solved numerically**: finite algorithms run to a stated resolution, which is the pixel doctrine in daily use, not the completed ℝ (Murphy's point).
3. **Idealization is harmless** (frictionless planes). *Reply:* agreed for (a); the objection is to *reifying* and to the unmarked "=". Whether that residual disagreement is theological or merely pedagogical is itself a question.
4. **Underdetermination.** A Christian can accept all of premises 1–5 and still use classical ℝ (many do). *Reply:* the claim is *fit*, and a reason to **hold the Reals under a name and a certificate**, not an entailment. The strong "should not" needs premise 2, which Scripture alone does not force.
5. **Constructive reals are still infinite objects.** A Cauchy sequence with a modulus is an infinite object. *Reply:* it is a **procedure** (finite rule plus modulus), potentially infinite; the vault accepts that, and the third leg ([[Creational Ultrafinitism]]) further limits scale.
6. **Circularity.** The argument assumes creaturely realism, whose own warrant is partly this. *Reply:* flag it; the independent support is steps 1, 5, 6.

## What is still missing

1. **A page defining "the Reals"** (axioms, constructions, the four meanings above) so the thesis has a precise target.
2. **Premise 2 argued out.** The number argument (every created multitude is numbered; every number is finite) needs a proper statement and an answer to the objection that "number" there means finite number by stipulation.
3. **The literature check** ([[Future Directions]] item 10): Christian authors who reject or limit actual infinity, for example Craig on actual infinites in reality (recollection), Bonaventure and Aquinas, Kronecker, Weyl, Poythress. Not verified; the novelty claim must not be made without it.
4. **Applied analysis**: differential equations and probability under the budget form. The largest gap between "approximate forms exist" and "the Reals are dispensable." Fourier is begun: [[Fourier Analysis]]; statistics is begun: [[Constructible Statistics]]; for differential equations the practice answer is that they are solved numerically, as pixels — what remains is the systematic budget-form account, not the existence of one.
5. **The divine-idea / created-quantity / construction tension** above.
6. **Pedagogy:** what a student at a Christian school is told about ℝ ([[Future Directions]] item 12).

## Honest summary

**The thesis needs only claim A.** The conclusion (do not *assert* the completed ℝ as a system of numbers; hold reals by named certificates) follows from the lack of warrant for a completed totality plus the certificate rule. The stronger claim B (the creation cannot contain an actual infinity) is Murphy's position and supports it, but is not needed for the conclusion ([[Theological Grounds]], "Two strengths of claim").

The case is **strong on the mathematics** (the pattern in the evidence file; Specker), **strong on the posture** (premises 1, 5, 6), and **strong internally** (steps 3–4). It is **moderate at the one step the Christian objection will aim at**: premise 2, that the creation contains no actualized infinity. The thesis that survives scrutiny today, in its ethical form, is that Christians ought not to claim to know what happens at infinity or to hold what they only imagine they can imagine, and in its mathematical form is: *Christians should not assert the classical completed Reals as a system of numbers; they should hold real numbers by named certificates, and treat the completed totality as, at most, in God's mind.* The stronger "no Christian may use ℝ" is not supported and is not Murphy's claim.
