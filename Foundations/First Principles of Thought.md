# First Principles of Thought

Logic begins from three principles taken as given, "the soil from which all reasoning grows":

1. **Identity.** $A$ is $A$. Whatever is, is itself.
2. **Non-contradiction (PNC).** Nothing can both be and not be in the same respect and at the same time.
3. **Excluded middle (LEM).** For any statement $P$, either $P$ or $\neg P$ is true.

They are called **axioms** because they cannot be proved by anything more basic: any attempt to prove them already presupposes them.

## Murphy's position on relaxing them

- **Relaxing a principle is not asserting that it is false.** To call LEM into question, or to throw it out and proceed anyway, is to ask what the principle *gives* us and what is possible without it. [[Three-Valued Logic]] does this for LEM and (in part) PNC; constructive systems such as Smooth Infinitesimal Analysis do it for LEM ([[Infinitesimals]]).
- **The principles are universals from "a God's eye point of view."** Through Scripture God discloses his point of view to us as he sees it. But we should *not* assume it is automatically possible for us: "Logic is not only a tool of certainty, but a means of examining the boundaries of certainty itself."
- **The third value is about the knower, not about truth.** "Truth remains One." Three-valued logic is "not a descent into relativism, but an admission of humility": the unknown is a place where our access stops, not a middle between true and false in reality. This is the same division as in [[Creaturely Realism]] (realist about the object) and [[Certificates]] (constructivist about access).
- **Logic is a normative science.** It does not describe how people in fact reason but how they ought to. It presupposes that truth is objective and partly accessible to the human intellect; it tests the *form* of reasoning and not the truth of premises. It is "the servant of truth, not its source."
- **Logos.** The word *logic* descends from the *Logos* of John 1:1. To think logically is to participate, in a small way, in the rational order that reflects God's own reason; the booklet will not treat logic as a symbolic game.

## How the principles fare in later systems

| principle | [[Boolean Algebra]] | [[Three-Valued Logic]] (K3) |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | holds | holds |
| LEM: $P\vee\neg P=1$ | holds | fails when $P$ is unknown |
| PNC: $P\wedge\neg P=0$ | holds | weakened to "never both true" |

*[AI]* Note: in K3 even $P\to P$ is unknown when $P$ is, so "identity" survives as the statement that $P$ is $P$, not as a theorem of the system. Where this leaves the schools of [[Schools Compared]] needs care and is left open.
