# Theological Grounds

[[Theology of Number]] collects the Scripture and theology already in the vault, mostly around One and Three, Babel and the redemptive frame. This page asks a different question: **for each mathematical commitment, what is the theological ground, how strong is it, and what is the objection?** All entries are **[AI]** proposals. Scripture is cited by reference, not quotation; the vault should pick one translation before quoting, and every reference and attribution below is to be checked.

## The method: four layers, and be honest about each

| Layer | What it is | Warrant |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Text** | What Scripture says | the strongest, but it is not a mathematics textbook |
| 2. **Doctrine** | Creation, God's incomprehensibility, the Creator-creature distinction, the Fall and its effect on knowing | the church's reading of layer 1 |
| 3. **Philosophy of knowing** | analogy, the actual/potential distinction, archetype and ectype | borrowed tools, weaker, argued rather than revealed |
| 4. **Mathematical commitment** | realism, certificates, creational ultrafinitism | what this vault asserts |

The honest claim is that layers 1 and 2 **motivate and fit** layer 4, and layer 3 does the real work of connecting them. They do not *prove* it: a Christian who accepts all of layers 1 and 2 can still hold classical set theory (many do). The vault's thesis is the weaker and defensible one, that its position is the **better fit**. State it so.

## Five finds worth developing [AI]

1. **"Innumerable" is relative to the counter.** Gen 15:5: Abraham is told to count the stars "if you are able." Gen 41:49: Joseph stores grain like the sand of the sea, "until he ceased to measure it, for it was beyond measure." Ps 147:4: God numbers the stars and names them. So Scripture's own usage is **resource-relative**: beyond number for Abraham, numbered by God. This is exactly the shape of [[Future Directions]] item 2c, and it is a text-level ground for the *graduated* skepticism in creational ultrafinitism, which needs no appeal to Aristotle.
2. **False balances and the equal sign.** Lev 19:35–36 and Prov 11:1, 20:10 condemn different weights for buying and selling. The vault's charge against standard analysis is that it "charges nothing for the move" from level 2 to level 11 ([[Certificates]]): using one "=" with different certificates is a false balance. A strong rhetorical and ethical anchor for the certificate discipline.
3. **Witnesses.** Dt 19:15 requires two or three witnesses before a charge stands. A certificate is a witness to an equality. (Analogy only.)
4. **Approximation that is improved, and a precision reserved.** 1 Cor 13:12: "now I know in part; then I shall know fully, even as I have been fully known." Knowing a number to $n$ places now, with an algorithm that can be pushed further, and God knowing all its digits, is the vault's picture of π ([[Creaturely Realism]]). It also gives the fifth posture of [[Five Acts]] (chastened doxology) a text.
5. **Counting as presumption.** David's census (2 Sam 24; 1 Chr 21) is judged. Reading is delicate (it is the motive that is condemned, not counting), but a theology of *numbering as an act of the creature before the Lord* is available: Ps 90:12, "teach us to number our days."

## Large finite and infinite in one epistemic category (Murphy)

Murphy's reading of Gen 15:5: Abraham cannot enumerate the stars even though their number is clearly finite, so a very large finite number sits **in the same category as infinity** for the creature. A kind of **biblical ultrafinitism**.

Stated so it keeps the text's own distinctions [AI]:

- **Epistemically**, the large finite and the infinite are *one class*: both are beyond the creature's counting. That class is defined by a **relation to a counter**, not by size, and it has no sharp edge, which is the graduated skepticism of [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] (and a text-level answer to "why not one more?").
- **Ontologically**, the text keeps them apart. The stars *have a number* (God numbers them, Ps 147:4); the infinity is God's understanding (Ps 147:5). The text collapses the finite and the infinite for **us** and for **no one else**.
- **What it forbids**: the inference "I cannot count it, so it is infinite" (or "so it is not a number at all"). The stars are neither.

One caution to state at once [AI]: the stars visible to the naked eye number a few thousand, which a person could count. So the verse cannot be read as a measured claim about what Abraham sees at night. It is better read as rhetoric under a promise, with the stars as the whole creation's stars (known to God), and the point is the **category**, not a magnitude. The vault should not rest the case on how many stars there are.

## Certificate constructivism: the biblical ground (second review)

A second review of this page finds good ground, with the right modesty: Scripture does not teach a mathematical constructivism; a **biblical epistemology** gives reasons to prefer exhibited, checkable warrant over appeals to completed infinities that cannot be exhibited or independently verified.

**Texts.** 1 Thess 5:21 (test everything), Acts 17:11 (the Bereans examined the Scriptures daily to see whether it was so; prestige of the speaker is no substitute), 1 Jn 4:1 (test the spirits). Add, from the map above, Dt 19:15 (witnesses) and Lev 19:35–36 / Prov 11:1 (honest weights). Translations differ on the wording of 1 Thess 5:21 ("prove", "test"); the vault should fix one. These establish an **epistemic posture**, not a formal system.

**The Principle of Exhibited Warrant** (adopted as a statement of the certificate rule; the review's wording, lightly edited): *a mathematical claim is established when a valid construction or proof can be exhibited and checked by explicit rules; an assertion that a result must occur at some unspecified stage of an unending process is not a substitute.* Two qualifications from the review that the vault should keep:

- A certificate may be a **proof or an algorithm**, not only a numerical witness. "Every integer above 1 has a prime factorization" is warranted by a finite induction ([[Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic]]), with no list of factorizations.
- **Constructivism is not ultrafinitism.** A proof can be finite in principle and too large for any person to inspect. Requiring *feasible* constructions is an extra thesis, the third leg ([[Creational Ultrafinitism]]), not part of constructivism.

**Worked case: "π contains the Bible."** The claim needs four things: the expansion never ends; every finite digit string occurs in it; a given string occurs at a finite position; the string means what the encoding says. None follows from "π is irrational and endless". The usual premise is that π is *normal* in base ten, which has **not been proved** (and irrationality does not imply normality). The vault's verdict is to ask for a certificate *per string*:

- **Certified, with a position:** the string 999999 first occurs at decimal place 762 (the Feynman point; places 762–767); computing 3,000 digits and reading them off confirms it. Anyone can recheck it.
- **Not certified:** "every string occurs", or "this long string occurs somewhere", with no position and no proof of normality.
- **Cost, if normality held (a heuristic, not a theorem):** a string of length $k$ would first appear after about $10^k$ digits, so a text of a few thousand characters would sit at a position vastly past the threshold of [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]: even granting the claim, the location would be notation, not a place.

**Where the review is weaker.** It starts from Heb 11:1–3 (faith's object is God, not an assertion of endlessness). That is true but does not bear on mathematical warrant; I would not lean on it. The testing texts do the work.

**A distinction to keep** [AI]. The vault accepts **proof by contradiction for a negative** ([[Certificates]]) and an existence claim only with a witness or a procedure. The review warns not to say indirect proof is always invalid; the vault's rule is narrower: *what does the proof entitle us to claim exists?*

## Three claims not to conflate

Taken from a second review of this page, and adopted because they sharpen the vault's own thesis:

| Claim | Kind | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Theological infinity**: God's fullness of being, wisdom, power, understanding | doctrine of God | Ps 90:2; Ps 145:3; Ps 147:5; Isa 40:28; Rom 11:33 |
| **Epistemic innumerability**: something exceeds a creature's capacity to enumerate | the creature's relation to what it knows | Gen 15:5; Gen 41:49; Rev 7:9 |
| **Mathematical potential infinity**: a finite procedure can be continued | how mathematics works | [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] |

The vault's thesis is that **innumerable to us is not infinite**: God numbers what Abraham cannot (Ps 147:4), so inability to complete a count does not show an actually infinite totality. The review's governing sentence: infinity belongs properly and essentially to God; the rule is available to us by successive finite acts, but a completed totality is a different ontological claim.

**Divine knowledge is not computational omniscience.** God does not traverse a sequence of counting operations to know a number; his knowledge is non-discursive. So "God knows all of π" ([[Creaturely Realism]]) is not "God has finished an infinite computation." That removes a tempting objection (that a God who knows infinitely many digits has completed an infinite process).

**A textual point to check [AI].** Ps 147:4 has God *numbering* the stars; v. 5 says of his *understanding* that it is "without number" or "beyond measure" (I recall the Hebrew as *ʾên mispār*; to check). Read together, the **stars** are numbered and the **understanding** is the thing without number: infinity is predicated of God, not of the created collection. That is the most direct text for the vault's ontology.

Further texts the review adds for the map: Isa 40:25–28 (he brings out the host by number and calls them by name, and his understanding is unsearchable), Job 38:4–7, Ps 90:2, Rom 11:33.

## The step the texts do not take: no actual infinity *in this world*

Murphy's position is that **no actualized infinity exists in the creation**. The review's caution is fair: Scripture does not say in so many words that every created collection is finite, and "God alone is infinite" does not by itself disprove an infinite creation. The doctrine of God has to be *developed* to reach it. Candidate arguments, from strongest to weakest [AI; all to be checked]:

1. **Every created thing is numbered by God, and every number is finite.** Ps 147:4; Mt 10:30 (the hairs of your head are all numbered); Isa 40:26; Job 28:25 (he apportions by measure). If a created multitude is *numbered*, it has a number, and a number is a finite thing. Aquinas, *Summa* I q.7 a.4 (recollection, to check), argues that every multitude is of some species of number and no number is infinite, so no actual infinite multitude. This is a **philosophical** argument from a **scriptural** datum, and the strongest available. Its weak point is the premise that "number" means finite number.
2. **Quality versus quantity.** God's infinity is of perfection and being (qualitative). If creaturely infinity were *quantitative*, one would still need a reason it counts as a rival. Here the creator-creature distinction is the reason offered: infinity is incommunicable. A reply is that a quantitative infinity is no divine attribute at all; the argument then needs argument 1.
3. **The temporal argument (Murphy; the traversal argument of Bonaventure, Philoponus and al-Ghazali, to check).** Everything in creation has a beginning (Gen 1:1; Jn 1:1; Ps 90:2); every growth in number takes time; there has been only finite time; so no growth can have reached an infinity. *The only way to be infinite would be to be made infinite.* This is a good argument against infinity **by accumulation**. It leaves three gaps, to be stated openly [AI]:
   - **Supertasks.** "Finite time, so finitely many steps" assumes each step takes at least some minimum time. If steps may shrink (half a minute, a quarter, …) infinitely many fit in finite time (Zeno). Excluding that is a claim about time or about physical acts (a minimum duration; about $10^{60}$ Planck times in the age of the universe, [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]), not about Scripture. *Murphy's reply:* created processes are temporally ordered, and being able to specify a process without stating its duration does not show that it completes in no time. That puts the **burden on whoever asserts a supertask**: a rule on paper is not a process that occurs. It is a sound defeater of the Zeno objection, though it does not prove that no supertask is possible.
   - **"Made infinite."** The argument concedes this option. Nothing in it shows that an infinite creature, made whole and not grown, is incoherent; that is what arguments 1 and 2 must show. Note that the saints' unending life is a *potential* infinity (never completed), which the argument does not touch.
   - **The continuum.** A created line is infinitely divisible, but divisible is not divided: on the Aristotelian reading (potential, never actual) the points are never a completed multitude ([[Future Directions]] item 8).
4. **Empirical.** Whether the cosmos is spatially infinite, or space a continuum, is not settled by Scripture, and the vault should say so. The physical bound of [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] is on *access*, not on a verdict about the whole.

### Two strengths of claim [AI]

Murphy's further statement rests on Westminster Confession 2.1 (God "infinite in being and perfection", and "immense" and "incomprehensible"; wording to check) with Shorter Catechism Q4: God alone is infinite in the fullness of his being; created things are dependent and not divine; whatever God creates is a determinate creaturely reality, not a completed infinity existing independently of his creative and sustaining act; so the unboundedness of a mathematical rule, and our ability to reason about it, do not warrant an actual infinity in creation.

Two claims are in that passage, and they differ in strength:

| | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **A (epistemic)** | We have **no warrant** to assert a completed infinity in creation: continuing a rule, or reasoning about it, does not show a completed totality | **Strong.** It follows from the confession, from creaturely dependence and from the certificate rule, and needs no further metaphysics |
| **B (metaphysical)** | A completed infinite quantity is **incompatible** with the determinate, creaturely character of created things | **Moderate.** It is the crux (premise 2) |

The step from the confession to **B** is where an objector will press. The confession says God is infinite in *being and perfection*, which is qualitative. "Determinate" and "bounded by its nature" are likewise qualitative: a thing has a definite kind. It does not follow that its *quantity* is finite, since a definite totality (the natural numbers, on the classical view) can be infinite. So "determinate" does not mean "finite" unless that is argued; this is the quality/quantity point of argument 2. B needs the number argument or the temporal argument, not the confession alone.

**Most important for the Reals thesis:** it needs only **A**. A completed totality may not be *asserted* without warrant, the burden is on whoever asserts it, and the vault's rule is to assert only what can be warranted ([[Christians and the Reals]]). The thesis does not have to prove B.

**Is "made infinite" coherent?** [AI] Not provably incoherent on logic alone; the case that it is, is cumulative: (i) Aquinas's number argument above, which works if "number" means what can be counted, and Cantor will reply that a transfinite cardinal is a number, so the meaning of the word *number* is again the crux ([[Number Hierarchy]]); (ii) a received being is limited by the kind of thing that receives it (Aquinas I q.7 a.2, to check), so infinity of *being* is God's alone, though this concerns being and not quantity; (iii) creation is described as ordered by "measure, number and weight" (Job 28:25; Wis 11:20) and as *finished*, "and all the host of them" (Gen 2:1), which suggests a determinate whole, though it is suggestive and not conclusive.

The honest status: **the rejection of actualized infinities is Murphy's position; it is motivated by the doctrine of God and best argued by (1); Scripture alone does not force it.** This also keeps the *mathematical* claims separate: the certificates, the potential infinity and the finite theorems do not depend on winning this ontological point.

## Counter-pressures to state fairly

- **Augustine, *City of God* XII.19** (to check): he argues that the series of numbers is infinite and yet comprehended by God's knowledge, so infinity is not beyond God. This is a classical Christian source *for* an infinite totality in God's mind. It fits [[Creaturely Realism]] (God knows all of π, we do not) and cuts against any claim that the infinite is *incoherent*; what the vault denies is that **creatures** possess it.
- **Canon.** Wis 11:20 ("measure, number, weight"), used heavily in the vault, is in the Apocrypha. Someone in the Reformed tradition will notice. Canonical parallels: Job 38; Isa 40:12; Ps 147.
- **Scripture rounds.** 1 Kgs 7:23 gives a basin ten cubits across and thirty around. The text states a measure to the precision its purpose needs, which is a small illustration of *precision relative to a budget*; it is not an error and not a theory of π.
- **Underdetermination.** See the method table: theology fits but does not force. Say *fit*.
- **Do not make Murphy a Platonist, formalist or intuitionist** ([[Theology of Number]] uses "creaturely realism").

## Philosophy layer: the tools

- **Archetypal and ectypal knowledge.** In Reformed scholasticism (Junius, to check) God's knowledge of himself is archetypal and ours of him ectypal, derived and fitted to the creature. Murphy's distinction between God's conceptions and numbers in the human sense ([[Creaturely Realism]]) has this structure; naming the source would give the vault a recognised vocabulary.
- **Analogy, not univocity.** Our knowledge is analogous to God's (Van Til, Frame, Poythress as in [[Theology of Number]]; to check).
- **Potential and actual.** The Aristotle–Aquinas distinction (*Physics* III; Aquinas *Summa* I q.7, to check) is already in [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]. Flag that it is a philosophical tool, not revelation.
- **Noetic effects of the Fall.** Our reasoning is finite by creation and impaired by sin; the vault so far uses only the first. A short note on whether the second adds anything mathematical is open.
