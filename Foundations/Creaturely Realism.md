# Creaturely Realism

**What numbers *are*.** The ontological leg of the three-part position (with [[Certificates]] for method and [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] for scale).

> Mathematical objects are **real** — they have natures we discover.
> They are **creational** — part of the ordered creation, not a self-existent Platonic realm.
> They are **creaturely-accessible** — finite minds know them by construction and approach, not by direct apprehension.

## The three moves

1. **Against nominalism and fictionalism.** The order is *given*, not invented, labeled, or fictional. Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness" has an answer: one Logos wrote both mind and world; the fit is *filial*, not lucky. (Maxwell's equations, 1865, predicted radio waves Hertz found in 1887 — labels don't do that; fictions don't hold up bridges.)
2. **Against Platonism and structuralism.** Numbers are not self-existent. God ordered all things "in measure, number, and weight" (Wisdom 11:20). The Author of mathematics is the Author of nature; a separate "number realm" is a fourth thing alongside God, matter, minds.
3. **The creaturely part.** God knows π completely, every digit, without convergence. We approach π by an algorithm that never arrives. That is not a defect — it is what it looks like for a *creature* to know a *created object* with a *finite mind*: by degrees, by construction, within the scale assigned.

## What each rival gets right (the "residue")

| Position | Right about | Wrong about (Murphy's reading) |
|---|---|---|
| Platonism | Mathematics is *discovered*; truth is necessary (2+2=4 couldn't have been otherwise) | Locates it in a self-existent realm; no Author; no account of how finite minds reach it |
| Nominalism | No spooky realm; number words track concrete collections | Cannot explain predictive success (Wigner); empties creation of Logos-structure |
| Fictionalism | Numbers aren't physical objects | "Not physical" ≠ "fictional" — creational in a different mode; fictions can be rewritten, the Pythagorean theorem can't |
| Structuralism | Relations are real; same structures recur in music, physics, architecture | Pushes the question back (where do the structures come from?); relations need relata |

## Mathematics as language (notation vs. truth)

$2^3=8$, $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$, $\log_2 8=3$ are **one truth in three notations** (the Confession: "three ways to write every mathematical sentence involving exponents"). Notations are contingent, historical, "ugly, creaturely"; the *relation* is not. "Dog," *canis*, κύων: three words, one creature.

**Analogical mathematics.** God has the Logos (language) and something like "heavenly math" — the rational order by which he structured creation. Ours is the *creaturely, analogical* version: mediated by contingent notation, partial, instrument-dependent. Classical analogical predication: God knows; we know — not in the same way. So Galileo ("mathematics is the language in which God wrote the universe") is an ontological claim, not a metaphor — and mathematical education is *formation*: learning to read one of the languages God wrote the world in.

## "Show me," and the exhibition of twoness

The vault's challenge to the Reals is *show me* ([[Exhibiting a Real]]). The demand is a blunt tool and not always what it says: **no one has ever seen twoness**. Twoness is not the word "two", not the symbol $2$, not anything else that can be set in front of you. Everyone has seen two people, two fingers; no one has seen twoness. Word and symbol *point at* the number; to confuse the pointer with the pointed-at is the confusion of map and land, and that confusion is very alive in our time. (That twoness is real although never seen is the realist side of the Platonism debate above; the vault keeps it, on creational grounds.)

So *show me* cannot mean *put the object itself in my hands* — no mathematical object is ever shown that way. What it honestly means is: **show me your certificate of access** — and access comes in degrees:

- **Complete exhibition.** Two fingers exhibit twoness *completely*: nothing about the number is left over, and the count is the certificate.
- **Partial exhibition.** Every exhibit of $\pi$ is partial: a symbol, which points, or digits, which stop. Murphy's convention makes the partiality explicit — write $\pi_2$ or $\pi_7$ for $3.14$ and $3.1415926$, and say plainly that $\pi_\infty$ is not accessible to us ([[Exact Until Output]]). God has $\pi_\infty$, by the third move above; we approach it.

The objection to the completed ℝ is therefore not *you cannot show me* — you cannot show me twoness either — but: **every exhibit you bring is partial, and the totality is billed as if it were shown.** Bare $\pi$, asserted as an exhibit, is the map billed as the land.

## One and many (from the 2020 essay)

The earliest statement in the corpus starts here: God is One and Three, a "numerical paradox"; we have knowledge analogous to his Oneness and to his Threeness but cannot reconcile them — so mathematics must begin with **epistemological humility**. See [[Theology of Number]].

## God's conceptions and the word "number"

[[SRC me.md Math Section]] says an object "exists only if it can be exhibited and certified." That is a claim about the word **number** in the *human* sense, not an anti-realist claim about what exists.

- God could conceive of something like a Real number. Humans cannot.
- While we are reasoning about **God's conceptions**, something like Cantor's diagonal argument makes some kind of sense ([[Infinity and Cantor]]).
- About **numbers in our human sense** it is incoherent.
- So the word *number* is limited to what a creature can exhibit and certify. Whatever only God could conceive is real, and is not a *number* in our sense. The word has to be used with care, as "star" had to be, and the [[Number Hierarchy]] is how.
