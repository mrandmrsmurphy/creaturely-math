unit

Every number, factored, exactly once

Each figure has exactly n dots, and the way they are grouped is the prime factorization of n — the only one it has.

Write n as a product of primes, largest first. The outermost layer is a ring of p points, where p is the largest prime. At each point sits a complete copy of the figure for n / p. Repeat until nothing is left to divide. A prime has no inner structure, so it stays a bare ring: 11 is eleven dots on a hendecagon. Composites nest: 10 is five pairs on a pentagon, and 12 is three copies of 4, each of them a pair of pairs — a square.

Long groups are laid along the radius, so the five pairs of 10 point out of the pentagon like petals. Round groups — squares, triangles, pentagons — are left upright, since turning them would gain nothing. The caption reads outside in, matching the layers you see.

Keys

  • Space — play or pause
  • ← → — one step back or forward
  • Shift + ← → — rewind or fast forward
  • G — show the grouping circles
  • Home — back to 1

Type a number in the corner and press Enter to jump there. Large primes need large rings, so the dots shrink: legibility gives out somewhere in the low hundreds, and a number like 9973 is a solid circle.
