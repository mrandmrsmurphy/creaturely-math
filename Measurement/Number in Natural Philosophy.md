# Number in Natural Philosophy

Where the mathematics-philosophy **meets measurement**. The Physics Textbook is the one place the position is taught *to students*.

## Quantity (ch. 7, "The Problem of Quantity")

- Aristotle, *Categories* VI: **discrete quantity** (number, speech) vs. **continuous quantity** (line, surface, time). Quantity has no contraries and no degrees: a number isn't "more two."
- "Number in mathematics is a creature of thought; number in science must describe something real."
- Number as **material cause** (how much — 3 kg of iron) and **formal cause** (structure/proportion — the same iron as a 10 cm sphere; 500 bricks vs. a wall 10 × 50).
- Epigraph: Augustine — "The numbers by which we count are not something we have made; rather, we see them and discern them."

## Modern confusions (the part closest to ultrafinitism)

School math teaches: $1=1.0=1.000$; units are optional; **precision is infinite**. Natural philosophy refuses all three: "We must not pretend to be able to know more than we can; we need **epistemological humility**." **Significant digits** are *measured or known precision*: 1 m, 1.0 m, 1.00 m differ in what is *claimed*. A scientific number = **value + unit + precision**. Accuracy (closeness to truth) vs. precision (detail; reproducibility). "Reflect on the moral dimension of precise measurement… truthfulness before God's created order."

This is a **finite-certificate** doctrine for physical numbers: a measurement *is* a certificate, with an error bound, and its digits stop where the evidence stops. Cf. [[Certificates]].

## Four faces of number (ch. 9, "All Numbers Are God's Numbers")

| | What it is | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical** | abstract, exact, timeless | "the perfect circle of Euclid exists only in thought; π can never be measured to its last digit" |
| **Scientific** | a measured quantity of a real thing; always carries uncertainty, always provisional | $d=vt$ tested against observation |
| **Philosophical** | what is number *in itself*? | Plato → nominalism; Christian: Trinity's unity/diversity; God "counts the stars and calls them by name" |
| **Computational** | finite, discrete, bounded, approximate | "the humblest of the four" — floating point |

"These four faces are not rivals, but companions." (Cf. Quadriga — [[Quadriga for Mathematics]].)

Measurement: "to bring the indefinite into proportion"; number is "always secondary to being" — it says how much, not what (formal cause) or why (final cause). **Quantophilia** warned against; "much in creation cannot be weighed, timed, or tallied — truth, goodness, beauty, love." The chapter's gloss on √2: some irrationals "represent *lengths* rather than countable quantities."

## History in the book

Plato (arithmetic "draws the soul toward being"; geometry over commerce), Archimedes' lever, Napier's logarithms, Mouton's proposal of a decimal measure from the meridian arc (a Christian origin story for the metric system), Galileo ("the book of nature is written in mathematical language"), Kepler ("Geometry is… a reflection from the mind of God"), Newton (one expression for the apple and the planets), Dirac ("God used beautiful mathematics in creating the world").

## Physically-grounded finitude (links to [[Creational Ultrafinitism]])

- **ch. 10, The Discarded Image:** the medieval cosmos was **finite, ordered, luminous**; "up" and "down" objective; the Ptolemaic model judged *reasonable* given available evidence — vs. the modern "flattened, mechanistic, infinite" cosmos.
- **ch. 18, Atoms:** Dalton's whole-number ratios (1:8, 3:4, 3:8) as evidence for "indivisible countable units, not continuous quantities"; discussion question on *Aristotle's matter as potentially infinitely divisible yet causally unified* and Brownian motion as a way to hold continuous and discrete together.
- **ch. 15, Action:** "working with *finite approximations* (sigma sums) that hint at this deeper reality."

*Candidate thesis for [[Future Directions]]:* a finite cosmos + countable atoms + finite precision is the *physical* basis for the numerical ceiling in [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] — and a reason the ceiling is the same *kind* of fact as a significant-digits limit.
