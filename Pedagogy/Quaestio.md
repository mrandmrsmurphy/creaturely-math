# Quaestio: posing the question rightly

> The first art of the [[Quadriga for Mathematics]]. *Quaestio* (Latin, "question, inquiry") is the art of deciding **whether, and how**, a problem admits symbolic treatment at all. Murphy: "*Is this a mathematical question at all?*" It is a Trivium-level act, done in plain language, before any symbol is written.

## Why it comes first

Most of reality is not subject to quantization. Applying symbolic method to what is not symbolic is a category error, and the habit of thinking that every problem yields to symbol-manipulation is Scientism's core mistake. So the move from words to numbers has to be **justified every time**. Quaestio is the gatekeeper, and it is the one art in which the Quadriga is openly *inferior* to the Trivium, because it is narrower in scope: love, beauty, moral obligation and the Trinity are not less real for being unmeasurable; they belong to other arts.

It is also a **permanent** discipline, not a beginner's step. The working mathematician returns to it: *Have I redefined the question mid-inquiry? Have I smuggled in unexamined conditions? Have I confused a model with reality?* The five offices are the "examined conscience" of the symbolic arts.

Theological ground: the *Logos* is prior to number (John 1:1). Posing a question well is a spiritual act, requiring humility, attention, and the recognition that the knower is a creature addressing a reality not of his making. Jesus often answered the question that should have been asked, not the one he was asked.

## The five offices, with their Latin pairs

| # | Office (English) | Virtue / vice (Latin) | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Notice** that a problem exists | *Agnitio / Negligentia*: recognition / negligence | Some of the best solutions came from someone seeing a problem others did not. |
| 2 | **Delineate** its boundaries | *Finitio / Indeterminatio*: definition / vagueness | Boundaries cure "the terror of the blank page". |
| 3 | **Decide** what to ignore | *Distinctio / Confusio*: distinction / confusion | Adding two flocks, the colour of the sheep does not matter. Keep separate things separate (a qualitative question from a quantitative one). |
| 4 | **Clarify the rules** of the system | *Conditio / Praesumptio*: qualification / presumption | A knight "moves to the other side": in L-shapes or not? State the conditions honestly; do not assume too much at the outset. |
| 5 | **Restate** it | *Ordinatio / Dissolutio*: ordering / dissolution | Draw a diagram, rephrase, turn words into symbols: whatever makes a first step possible. |

*[AI]* The source pairs the English offices with the Latin pairs by position. Offices 1, 2 and 4 match their Latin names closely; 3 ("decide what to ignore") and 5 ("restate") match *Distinctio* and *Ordinatio* more loosely, and Murphy may want to say which Latin gloss he intends.

These offices ensure the inquirer does not rush to *Praxis* or *Apodeixis* with a malformed question. Murphy calls them the **moral epistemology of the threshold**.

## Its ancestry

Aquinas's *quaestio disputata* (objections, *sed contra*, *respondeo*, replies) is the ancestor: "the well-formed question is prior to the well-formed answer." Murphy keeps the seriousness (hear the objections, submit to the question before pronouncing) and changes three things: the **medium** (the threshold between word and symbol, not words alone), the **telos** (creaturely knowing in all its forms, including "What is the gravitational constant?" and "How do I build this?", not contemplative theology alone), and the **anthropology** (no ranking of contemplative over active knowing). It also extends Pólya's first stage, *Understanding the Problem* (what is the unknown, the data, the condition?), by the question Pólya assumed: *is this a symbolic problem at all?*

## In this vault's mathematics [AI]

Each of these is a place where the vault's own pages already do what a Quaestio office asks.

- **Notice, delineate: "what kind of equality?"** Murphy's own diagnosis of the viral "$1+2+3+\cdots=-\tfrac1{12}$" video is that it fails the office *Clarify the rules*: it never says which equality is in use ([[Equal Signs]], [[Number Hierarchy]], levels 13 and 14).
- **Finitio / Indeterminatio: define "number".** "Every **number** has a unique prime factorization" is a theorem only if *number* is defined; the 10^200+23 challenge is a Quaestio question ([[Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic]]).
- **Distinctio / Confusio: Numbers, Lengths and Pixels.** Keeping the kinds of thing apart is the office; collapsing levels 7 to 12 into one object, $\mathbb R$, is the confusion ([[Numbers, Lengths and Pixels]], [[Number Hierarchy]]).
- **Conditio / Praesumptio: the hypotheses of a theorem.** The exact intermediate value theorem presumes a completed continuum and a continuity with no stated modulus; the approximate form states its condition (the modulus $\delta(\varepsilon)$) as an input ([[Intermediate Value Theorem]], hard problem 1).
- **Decide what to ignore: the model.** A measured length is a number *and* a tolerance; which one the question wants is Quaestio's call ([[Number in Natural Philosophy]], [[Exact Until Output]]).
- **"Is this a counting problem?"** The source's example of Quaestio in first grade. It is also the first question of [[Probability]] and [[Constructible Statistics]]: are the objects finite and countable, so that exact counts answer it?
- **Quaestio against the real line.** [[Scale and Bands]] asks Quaestio's question of physics: have we mistaken the continuity of our notation for the continuity of nature?

## Failure modes

- **Quaestio skipped:** applies mathematical tools to non-mathematical questions (source).
- **Quaestio assessed:** by asking which tool, if any, is appropriate, including problems whose right answer is "this is not a mathematical question". The hardest of the four arts to grade formally; it can be assessed through discussion (source).
