# Proof Types, by grade — with a certificate audit

The planning draft names a **Big Six** curriculum spine — *Show it · Check all · Follow it · Break it · Turn it · Extend it* — expanding to **eleven** types with grade bands and a virtue each. The Quadriga column is the planning draft's own tag ([[Quadriga for Mathematics]]); the audit column is mine: *does this proof form sit comfortably with [[Certificates]] and [[Creational Ultrafinitism]]?* **The grade bands are a plan; none has been taught as a year of school, and working them out is future pedagogical work.**

| # | Type | Grades | Virtue | Quadriga | Audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demonstration / exhibition ("Here it is.") | K–2 | the dignity of operative knowing | Praxis: making the thing is the proof | ✔ the gold standard |
| 2 | Exhaustion / enumeration | 2–5 | completeness | Quaestio: the exhaustiveness check | ✔ finite, decidable — the model of a *bounded* quantifier |
| 3 | Direct proof | 5–8 | linear clarity; trust in the chain | a clean Q→N→P→A | ✔ |
| 4 | **Proof by construction** ("To prove possible, build it.") | 5–9 | "wisdom as making" — deeply biblical; Praxis → Apodeixis | Praxis elevated to Apodeixis | ✔ certificate = the object |
| 5 | Counterexample | 5–8 | humility; precision | Noesis 7, Logical extremes | ✔ a finite certificate |
| 6 | Contrapositive | 8–10 | flexibility ("the same truth from the other end") | Noesis 11, Reframing | ✔ logical equivalence, no new commitments |
| 7 | Cases / strategic partition | 8–10 | insight | Noesis 4, Break it up | ✔ if finitely many |
| 8 | **Mathematical induction** | 9–11 | "trust in a recursive process; humility about infinite cases" | Noesis 8, Induction | ⚠ proves a claim for *all* $n$ — including those past any feasible bound. Reading as *schema*: "for any $n$ you can name, the step can be performed" (potential) vs. as a claim about a completed ℕ (actual). See [[Future Directions]] item 6. |
| 9 | **Contradiction** | 10–12 | courage: hold a false assumption without flinching | Noesis 7, Logical extremes | allowed when proving a **negative** (no $a/b$ squares to $2$), which is constructively valid. Not valid to *conclude an existence*: the $\sqrt2$ proof shows there is no ratio, not that an irrational number exists. |
| 10 | Extremal / minimal counterexample | 11–12 | patience | Noesis 3, Specialization | ⚠ well-ordering is induction in disguise |
| 11 | Invariant / monovariant | 11–12 | perception of deep structure; bridge to natural philosophy | Noesis 10, Symmetry | ✔ finite-state; conservation laws |

## Pedagogical notes carried over

- **Counterexample early:** children overgeneralize ("all primes are odd" → 2); one case breaks a universal claim — "intellectual humility that transfers."
- **Induction too early:** often taught mechanically in Algebra II before the *recursion/inheritance* idea is in place — "symbol manipulation, not proof."
- **Contrapositive vs. contradiction** are conflated at serious cost: contrapositive = *Reframing*; contradiction = *Logical Extremes*. Teach the first first.
- **Construction is the biblical flavor:** Adam was a gardener; Jesus a *tekton*.
