# Praxis: faithful execution

> The third art of the [[Quadriga for Mathematics]]. *Praxis* (Greek πρᾶξις, "action, deed, practice") is the disciplined **doing** of what *Quaestio* has posed and *Noesis* has seen: the measurement, the computation, the manipulation of symbols. Murphy anchors the name in the *Praxeis Apostolōn*, the Acts of the Apostles: the early Church did not stay in the upper room but went out to do the work. Knowledge must "get off its duff and measure".

## The unique claim

In language, thinking and saying are continuous: to think the argument is already to begin stating it. In symbolic work, **understanding and executing are separable**. A student can ask the right question, see the right pattern, and still lose a subscript, misapply an algorithm, or slip in the arithmetic. That failure is "not cognitive, it is *praxial*": a distinct category of error, taught and assessed separately. Only for God are word and action the same.

So *Praxis* is "the unique contribution" of the Quadriga, and the reason people "mistake the unique part for the whole" and confuse **mathematics with computation**. In [[Three Tiers]] arithmetic *is* Praxis: the computation of calculable values by exact algorithms within a stated system, whose success is correctness.

## Why it is a full art, not a lesser one

The classical hierarchy ranked the mechanical (*banausic*) arts as servile because they served the body, and ranked theory above practice. Murphy's reversal rests on the **tekton** (Jesus, *ho tektōn*, the carpenter, "lacked nothing of full humanity"): to denigrate technical and manual knowledge is to denigrate the humanity Christ assumed. If a blacksmith's work glorifies God as truly as a monk's meditation, "the knowledge embedded in that work cannot be intellectually inferior." Historical warrant: thermodynamics followed the steam engine; the cathedral masons built for generations without a structural theory. "Truth is multigenerational, and the doing of one generation becomes the understanding of the next." In the framework's wording this makes Praxis "equal in dignity to insight and demonstration", and it also protects against the Gnostic contempt of the material ([[Greek Mathematics]], [[Mathematics 500 to 1500]], [[Corrective Narrative]]).

Also: *homo faber* is a mode of *homo adorans*, not a rival. Six days of labour are sanctified by the one of rest, so the six were never profane. "**Speed is not virtue.** Execution is a school of the soul": the student who works slowly and faithfully is practising *Fiducia* and *Docilitas* in a way the quick and careless one is not.

## The five regulae, with their Latin pairs

| # | Rule | Virtue / vice (Latin) | Murphy's gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Consistency** | *Fiducia / Inconstantia* (confidence / inconstancy) | Carry the chosen method faithfully to its end. "The spirit is willing but the flesh is weak." The discipline of perseverance (Gal 6:9). |
| 2 | **Verify** | *Verificatio / Praecipitatio* (verification / precipitation) | Check, re-check, triple check: "for want of a nail". |
| 3 | **Be correctable** | *Docilitas / Pertinacia* (teachability / stubbornness) | Perseverance is not pig-headedness. Willingness to be corrected by evidence, the community, or a better method. |
| 4 | **Stay mindful** | *Mensura / Superstitio* (measure / superstition) | Trusting a process is not forgetting *why* it works. Proportion confidence to what the execution warrants. The vice is **cargo-cult mathematics**. |
| 5 | **Remember why** | *Ministerium / Mechanismus* (service / mechanism) | The work is for love of God and neighbour. The vice is to serve the method and treat the human as the machine's extension. |

On the last one, the source remarks that "robot" comes from the Czech word for "slave". The Czech *robota* is forced labour or drudgery (the word *robot* entered English through Čapek's play *R.U.R.*, written 1920; recollection, to check), which is the point Murphy wants and a slightly more accurate statement of it.

The regulae are also "maxims for the Christian life": the young inquirer's fire can cool into routine, and Praxis is "the art of not growing weary". And it is humble action: "neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but only God who gives the growth" (1 Cor 3:7).

## In this vault's mathematics [AI]

- **Consistency.** The approximate intermediate value theorem is Praxis done to the end: nine bisection steps for $x^2-2$ on $[1,2]$, each step exact, no step skipped or improvised ([[Intermediate Value Theorem]]). So are the rows of a difference table ([[Discrete Calculus]]) and the arithmetic of [[Tally Marks]], the earliest Praxis, where addition is concatenation and subtraction is removal.
- **Verify.** The cheapest check in arithmetic is a clock: casting out nines. $123\times457=56211$; mod 9, $123\equiv6$ and $457\equiv7$ and $6\cdot7=42\equiv6$, and $56211\equiv6$. The check does not prove the product right, but a mismatch proves it wrong. This is one more reason the vault treats clock math as fundamental as the four operations ([[Modular Arithmetic]], [[Historical Order]]).
- **Docilitas.** The vault's own working rule is to fix a verified error where it stands and flag, not smooth over, a hard problem ([[Future Directions]]). The history pages record how often published mathematics needed correction ([[Mathematics 1900 to Today]]).
- **Mensura / Superstitio.** The significant-digit discipline of [[Number in Natural Philosophy]] is *Mensura* in a single rule. Its mathematical twin is the rule of [[Exact Until Output]]: do not store the decimal as if it were the number; a decimal that cannot go backwards into $\pi$ was trusted without knowing why it worked.
- **Mechanismus.** A proof checked by a program is a gain in *Verificatio*; to treat the checked proof as the end, and the person as its operator, is the vice ([[Mathematics 1900 to Today]]: "the checker cannot certify itself").
- **Praxis before Apodeixis in teaching.** Euclid in the freshman year, each proposition *done* with compass and straightedge before it is read as proof ([[Greek Mathematics]], [[Curriculum Plan]]); a logarithm table made by hand before logarithms are named ([[The Road to Calculus]]).

## Failure modes

- **Praxis without Noesis:** can integrate by parts but cannot see why the technique applies (the student was never taught to *see*).
- **Noesis without Praxis:** good intuitions and constant arithmetic errors; execution needs separate practice.
- **Assessment:** Praxis is assessed by execution on *known* problem types under conditions that reward carefulness over speed (source). Noesis, by contrast, needs novel problems.
- **Arithmetic versus mathematics (Murphy).** Arithmetic is a skill, a technical performance: it can be drilled, and timed fluency checks belong there (the K–5 fluency goal of [[Historical Order]]). Mathematics proper is an art and a science: it cannot be drilled or timed. The timed test is not the enemy of the Quadriga; it is simply the arithmetic room, not the mathematics room.
