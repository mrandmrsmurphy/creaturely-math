# The Quadriga Scientiae, applied to mathematics

> Four **arts** of symbolic knowledge: **[[Quaestio]]** (pose the question rightly), **[[Noesis]]** (see how the symbols relate), **[[Praxis]]** (do the work faithfully), **[[Apodeixis]]** (show the result by necessity). The Trivium masters the **Word**; the Quadriga masters the **Symbol**, always under the prior lordship of the *Logos*: John 1 does not say "In the beginning was the Algorithm."

The Quadriga began as a separate project, Murphy's answer to the question of how to teach STEM in classical Christian education. This page documents it as it stands and **what it says about mathematics**. [[Quadriga and the Position]] joins it to the three-part position of the rest of the vault.

## The idea in one paragraph

The classical Quadrivium (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) sorts its four arts by the **modes of number**: number in itself, in space, in time, in space and time (Stratford Caldecott's two-axis grid is the most compelling statement). After 2500 years of mathematics there are many more axes along which number can be extended, and the question *what are the fundamental disciplines that make any practice of mathematics possible?* has changed. The Quadriga sorts the same territory by **epistemological function**, by what a mind does when it handles symbols, and says there are four such acts. Its unifying medium is "formal symbol": mathematics, logic, musical counterpoint, even computer science. Its key claim: in linguistic work thinking and saying are continuous, but in symbolic work **understanding and execution are separable**, so a distinct art of faithful doing (*Praxis*) is needed. That art is the Quadriga's special contribution, and its neglect is why mathematics keeps being confused with computation.

## Where it comes from

Pólya's four stages in *How to Solve It* (1945) are "the direct seed":

| Pólya | Quadriga art | What the Quadriga adds |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding the Problem | **Quaestio** | the prior question Pólya assumed, *is this a symbolic problem at all?* (the anti-Scientism safeguard); five offices with Latin virtue/vice pairs |
| Devising a Plan | **Noesis** | Pólya's heuristics become the [[Twelve Exercises]], as virtues and not tips, with Latin names and theological grounding; Rest added (from Poincaré's anecdote) |
| Carrying Out the Plan | **Praxis** | the dignity of operative knowing (the *tekton*), five regulae as maxims of the Christian life |
| Looking Back | **Apodeixis** | reflection recast as *demonstration*: the Sabbath rest of inquiry; four virtutes, with the anti-rhetorical function named |

Murphy's formulation: "Pólya observed what mathematicians do. The Quadriga names why it is right for creatures made in the image of God to do it that way." Pólya in turn drew on Descartes's *Regulae*, Leibniz, Bolzano and Aristotle's *Topics*; the Quadriga "receives Pólya as he received his sources: gratefully, with extension and theological grounding." What the Quadriga adds, in the list the wiki gives of Murphy's own contributions: the threshold question; the Word/Symbol distinction; the theological warrants; the status of a liberal art (a curriculum, not a teacher's guide); and the Latin virtue/vice pairs.

## The name

*Quadriga* is the four-horse chariot of antiquity, and the medieval name for the **fourfold sense of Scripture** (the *Quadriga Religiosa* or *Religionis*): literal or historical, allegorical, tropological or moral, anagogical. The *Quadriga Scientiae* is its counterpart for the **Book of Nature**: both books have the same Author, and the same fourfold shape of mind reads both. *Scientiae* is *scientia* in the pre-modern sense, systematic knowledge as a cultivated habit of mind, not "science" in the modern sense (Peter Harrison's recovery of *scientia* and *religio* as virtues never in competition is the historical warrant).

| The medieval sense of Scripture | The art | The parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Literal / historical | Quaestio | receive what is given before interpreting it |
| Allegorical | Noesis | the creative leap; what lies beneath the surface |
| Tropological (what to do) | Praxis | formation through right action; "execution is a school of the soul" |
| Anagogical (where you are going) | Apodeixis | "*Q.E.D.* is a little eschatology" (Murphy's own link) |

The table follows the wiki's reading of Hugh of St Victor's *Didascalicon*, where the liberal arts and the four senses live together as two halves of one education; only the Apodeixis row is Murphy's own words. **One difference** the source names honestly: the four senses are *simultaneous lenses* on a finished text, while the four arts are a *sequence* in an unfinished inquiry. (Murphy's Part I speaks of "existing quadrigas — such as Luther's". Luther in fact favoured the literal sense over the fourfold method, so the attribution is flagged for Murphy.)

## Word and Symbol

| | **Word** (Trivium, *Logos*) | **Symbol** (Quadriga) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | natural sign, part of living language | artificial, conventional sign |
| Meaning | rich, connotative, ambiguous (fruitful in poetry, law, theology) | precise within the system, largely denotative |
| Thinking and doing | nearly continuous | separable |
| Truth mode | persuasion, coherence, plausibility | necessity, consistency within the rules |
| Failure mode | misinterpretation, rhetorical manipulation | calculation error, symbol drift, cargo-cult use |
| Biblical resonance | *Logos* (John 1) | "by number, weight, and measure" |

A word participates in the richness of reality as met by persons; a symbol is "a tool deliberately stripped of that richness so it can be manipulated with mechanical reliability". As the 2020 essay puts it: mathematics is the language of **things**; English (or Hebrew or Swahili) is the language of **persons**. Hence the Quadriga is **subordinate** to the Trivium: mathematical structure is discovered and communicated *through* language, even if it outruns language once formalised. The entry to the symbolic arts is a plain-language question, *is this a mathematical question?*, a Trivium-level act. A student doing mathematics sets aside a word's richness for a symbol's power, and must be taught to pick the richness up again afterwards: "show your work **and** explain what it means." ([[Three Tiers]] draws the line between arithmetic, mathematics and metamathematics; the Word/Symbol line is a separate cut.)

## The four arts at a glance

| Art | The act | Latin virtues (pairs) | Safeguard against | Page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quaestio** | pose the question rightly | five officia: *Agnitio, Finitio, Distinctio, Conditio, Ordinatio* | Scientism: symbol-method on non-symbolic things | [[Quaestio]] |
| **Noesis** | see how the symbols relate | twelve exercitia: *Similitudo … Incubatio* | the reduction of mathematics to mere Praxis | [[Noesis]], [[Twelve Exercises]] |
| **Praxis** | execute faithfully | five regulae: *Fiducia, Verificatio, Docilitas, Mensura, Ministerium* | mathematics as mere computation; contempt of the material (Gnosticism) | [[Praxis]] |
| **Apodeixis** | show by necessity | four virtutes: *Recensio, Perspicuitas, Necessitas, Imitabilitas* | rhetoric standing in for proof | [[Apodeixis]] |

Murphy: "Without *Noesis*, *Quaestio* is merely cautious, *Praxis* merely mechanical, and *Apodeixis* merely formal." And *Quaestio* is the one art in which the Quadriga yields to the Trivium: "the Quadriga is truly inferior to the Trivium, because it is much more limited in scope."

## Sequence and the twelve edges

The forward order **Quaestio → Noesis → Praxis → Apodeixis** is the *direction of aspiration*, and the order of authority: the form of the question constrains the insight, and the standard of proof is fixed by the question. Real inquiry travels all twelve possible directed routes. The source's own account:

| Kind | Edges | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Forward** | Q→N, N→P, P→A | the usual build |
| **Skip-forward** | Q→P, Q→A, N→A | "what is this ratio?" demands measurement; the question type sets the standard of proof |
| **Backward** (correction) | N→Q, P→Q, A→Q | attempting insight shows the question was malformed; execution breaks the question; a finished proof reveals the question was a special case of something larger (Fermat's Last Theorem leading to new questions about elliptic curves) |
| **Retrograde illumination** | P→N, A→N, A→P | measuring Tycho's data *produced* the insight of elliptical orbits; the finished proof reveals a cleaner path than the one that found it (Euclid); execution that will not converge shows Apodeixis is not yet reached. Murphy's example of Praxis returning to Noesis: execution begins and "reveals the problem is computationally intractable (NP-hardness, the age of the universe)". |

The theological root is the Hamartological mark: because we are finite and our intuitions about creation are unreliable, we cannot complete the forward arc cleanly in one pass. The correction loops are the world pushing back; the retrograde illuminations are the finished proof showing what could not be seen from inside the inquiry. "The sequence is the direction of aspiration; the twelve edges are the map of how creatures actually get there."

*[AI]* A mathematical specimen of each family: **forward** (a lesson on $7^{10}\bmod5$: question, the cycle $2,4,3,1$, the calculation, the proof by period 4); **backward** (finding that $\sqrt2$ "exactly" has no ratio changes the question, [[Diagonal Argument]]); **retrograde** (the hand-made logarithm table, Praxis, reveals the exponent law, Noesis, [[The Road to Calculus]]); and Murphy's own **P→N intractability** is the bridge to the vault's ultrafinitism ([[Creational Ultrafinitism]]).

## Mathematics is not computation

"People often confuse *mathematics* with *computation*… they mistake the unique part for the whole." Praxis is the part the humanities lack, and the part that "gets mistaken for the whole" in mathematics education. In [[Three Tiers]] arithmetic is Praxis; mathematics proper is the work of the other three arts as much as of Praxis; metamathematics is the discipline of keeping Quaestio honest.

In mathematics, Noesis precedes Praxis ("a student who executes without seeing has learned a procedure, not mathematics"). The inversion that is routine in an experimental science, where the doing teaches the seeing, is rarer here but real; the Pythagoreans were shocked by the irrational, and mathematics teachers should *seek* such moments and let a student try both $(3+4)^2$ and $3^2+4^2$ before being told.

## Scale: the creature in the middle

The Quadriga's *Praxis* (a creature doing real work with real instruments) and *Quaestio* (can this question be put at all?) both presuppose a **place**. Humans are set in a universe larger and smaller than themselves, slower and faster, beyond what the unaided senses reach: what takes centuries or a fraction of a millisecond, what is wider than the field of vision or finer than a hair's width. Mathematics, with tools, makes the invisible visible, "but it is not a passport to cease to be created creatures": the reach is bought with instruments, tolerance and cost. That is the companion thesis to the Quadriga's humility, and it is stated in [[Scale and Bands]] (the band table and the band-gap principle belong to the sciences and are not developed here). In the vault it is the physical face of the finite budget of [[Creational Ultrafinitism]] and of the rule that *a measured number is a number with a tolerance* ([[Number in Natural Philosophy]]).

## Failure modes and assessment

The source's diagnostic for a teacher: each art has a separate failure and a separate fix.

| Failure | What it looks like | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Praxis without Noesis** | can integrate by parts, cannot see why the technique applies | never taught to *see* |
| **Noesis without Praxis** | good intuitions, constant arithmetic errors | execution requires its own practice |
| **Quaestio skipped** | mathematical tools applied to non-mathematical questions | the gatekeeper was never installed |
| **Apodeixis absent** | reports the answer, cannot explain why it must be true | demonstration never taught as distinct from computation |

"Why is this kid getting Bs on tests but can't solve anything novel? Praxis without Noesis." Assessment, in the source's words: **Noesis** by novel problems the memorised algorithm cannot solve; **Praxis** by execution on known problem types under conditions that reward carefulness over speed; **Quaestio** by asking which tool, if any, is appropriate, including problems whose right answer is "this is not a mathematical question"; **Apodeixis** by proof-writing, oral explanation or teaching-back. Use separate rubrics for N, P and A; Quaestio is hardest to grade and can be assessed through discussion. The curriculum diagnostic the source offers: a curriculum can be placed on four sliders (Grammar ↔ Quaestio, Logic ↔ Noesis, ∅ ↔ Praxis, Rhetoric ↔ Apodeixis); the examples it gives from the field (Saxon as Praxis-heavy and Noesis-light; the Art of Problem Solving as Noesis-heavy) are Murphy's impressions and are not verified here.

## The theology

The framework is warranted by eight marks (the draft for Catholic readers re-grounds them). Two carry most of the weight for mathematics: **Christocentric** (the *tekton*; denigrating technical knowledge denigrates the humanity Christ assumed) and **Hamartological** ("I am not trustworthy alone"; the Fall yields community, so proof must be checkable by others). All eight, with where each lands in the vault, are in [[Quadriga and the Position]]. The **[[Four Kinds of Truth]]** (revelation, properly basic, logical, verisimilitude) belong to the Hamartological mark: the fourth, which includes everything built on observation, is the most provisional, and that is why inquiry must be multi-generational.

## Future pedagogical work

None of the following has been taught as a year of school. It is listed so that it is not lost.

1. **Proof types by grade.** The eleven types carry grade bands (K–2 up to 11–12) in the planning draft ([[Proof Types]]). That sequence is a plan, to be worked out and tried.
2. **Each exercise of the Twelve in a classroom.** A set of problems per exercise, from this vault's mathematics; the worked examples in [[Twelve Exercises]] are a start.
3. **Rubrics.** Separate rubrics for Noesis, Praxis and Apodeixis, and a way to assess Quaestio.
4. **A curriculum placement chart.** Where the common mathematics curricula fall on the four sliders, checked and not impressionistic.
5. **A unit template for mathematics.** Quaestio prompts, the Exercises a unit will name, a "surprise" moment where Praxis precedes Noesis, and a capstone demonstration that is "not 'I computed the right answer' but 'here is why this must be so'".
6. **The book.** *What is Mathematics*, Part III, "Walking in Math", is organised by the four arts and is still empty ([[Curriculum Plan]], [[Future Directions]]).
7. **A teaching sequence aimed at teachers.** A three-day equipping sequence exists in the source (Day 1: Word and Symbol, the marks, the four arts, the sliders; Day 3: a lesson template and the failure-mode taxonomy). It is written for science and mathematics together.
